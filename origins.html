D'ALESSIO ORIGINS

From Torricella Sicura to Cornwall

The D'Alessio family on this tree comes from Torricella Sicura, a small town in the province of Teramo, in the Abruzzo region of Italy. Alfred D'Alessio left with an Italian passport issued in October 1923, settled in Cornwall, Ontario, and married Simone Hébert. This page gathers what the family album and public records tell us. Where something is still a guess, we say so.

Torricella Sicura

A typed note kept in the family album names the town and Alfred's parents. Alfred's 1987 obituary gives his mother's full name: Rosaria Cappeli. The album calls her Rosario.

D'Alessio partial Family Tree:
In Torricella Sicura, Province de Teramo, in Italy from the Abruzzi Area, Vincent D'Alessio and his Wife Rosario, had four sons and Two daughters. From this family Comes our Family.

Some day Someone may go to Italy and the Family Tree could be increased With information prior to our clan. Relatives are there to this day.

Typed note, family album

Four sons and two daughters

The album names all six of Vincent and Rosaria's children, which matches the typed note exactly. Alfred's 1987 obituary confirms every one of them.

Sons
Alfred; Sistino, still in Italy in 1987; Edward, still in Cornwall in 1987; and Augustine, who died before Alfred (spelled Sistine and Agustine in the album)
Daughters
Giovanno and Annie, both of whom died before Alfred (Giovanno on the photo label, Giavanni in the obituary; Giovanna is the usual Italian spelling)

Both this photo and the one above are dated 1916, but Vincent looks much older in the first. One of the two dates may be off, which is common with labels added years later.

Augustine used to be the open question. He stood with Alfred and Edward at the wedding, and his son Norman is called a cousin of Alfred's boys. Alfred's obituary settles it: he was "predeceased by one brother Augustine D'Alessio".

Alfred was about 23. Italians heading to Cornwall at the time usually landed in Halifax and travelled on by rail, finding work on the railways, the canal and in construction. Families tended to follow a first arrival from the same town, and that's what the album shows: by the time Alfred married, his brothers Augustine and Edward were in Canada too.

Passport read from a photo of the album page; some handwriting is hard to make out. Context: Wikipédia (French); Canadian Museum of Immigration at Pier 21

Updated. This page first guessed that the family arrived around 1900, going by Alfred's birth year. The passport shows Alfred left Italy himself, as an adult, in October 1923 or soon after. His middle initial V. most likely comes from his father's name, Vincent (Vincenzo in Italian). The Augustine line, first listed as a separate family, turned out to be Alfred's brother's.

Simone

Simone Hébert was born in 1907. Her parents were Alpheda Simpson (spelled Alfida in Simone's obituaries) and Joseph Domina Hébert. Her mother died in 1912 in Hull, Quebec (now part of Gatineau), the same year as Simone's photo at age 5. Her father, Domina, died in 1930 at 56.

The wedding date isn't written down, but it can be narrowed. It came after Alfred's passport was issued in October 1923, and before Domina died in 1930. Their first son, Vincent, was born in 1928, so the wedding was most likely between 1924 and 1928.

Alfred and Simone's last years

Alfred and Simone lived at 331 Sixth Street East in Cornwall. Alfred died at the Hotel Dieu Hospital on January 28, 1987, at 86. His funeral was a Mass of the Resurrection at St. Columban's Church, and he was held in the Notre Dame Vault until a spring burial in the parish cemetery. Simone moved to St. Joseph's Villa and died at the Hotel Dieu on October 29, 1990, at 83. Her funeral was also at St. Columban's.

The boys

Norman's caption was the first clue that Augustine was Alfred's brother. Alfred's 1987 obituary later confirmed it.

It was a big year for Gerald. He married Lorraine Ruest on February 27, 1954, with his little brother Freddy in the background of the photo. Their family is the branch of the tree that leads to Mark, and to Cris.

Vince, 1928–2001

  • Alfred and Simone's son Vincent, known as Vince, was a retired civil servant known for his love of music.
  • He died at the Ottawa General Hospital on February 12, 2001, at 72, after 38 years of marriage to Sylvia (née Dickenson).
  • His obituary lists his brothers and where they lived in 2001: Gerry in Russell, Robert (Bob) in Smiths Falls, and André and Fred in Cornwall.
  • The Royal Canadian Legion, Branch 297, held a Legion tribute for him.

The name in Italy today

D'Alessio means "of Alessio" (Italian for Alexis or Alexander). The surname is found mostly in central and southern Italy, which includes Abruzzo.
