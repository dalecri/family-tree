    HOW THIS TREE WORKS

    Reading the tree

    The tree grows the way a real one does: the oldest generation is at the bottom, and each generation grows one level higher. Poppy is the trunk.

    Every fork in a branch is a couple. Each partner sits on their own twig, and their children's branches grow up out of that fork.

    Ink branch: a confirmed link. Pencil dashes: we think so, but it still needs confirming. Empty bud: someone we know belongs here but haven't added yet. Polaroid: a photo, or initials until we have one.

    Getting around

    • Move: drag the page. On a phone, drag with one finger.
    • Zoom: scroll, pinch, or use the + and − buttons. Fit shows the whole tree again.
    • Meet someone: tap their name. Their card shows dates, a short story, and their parents, partners and children. Tap any of those names to jump to that person.
    • Find someone: type a name in the search box and press Enter. If they're inside a folded branch, it opens for you.
    • Follow a line: when you tap someone, everyone outside their line (parents, grandparents, partner, children and grandchildren) fades back so their branch stands out.
    • Fold a branch: tap the small − pill under any couple to tuck their descendants away. It turns into + and a count; tap it again to open that family one generation at a time. Very big families open with the younger branches folded.
    • Collapse everything: the button under Fit folds every branch back to the first families in one tap. Tap it again to open them all.
    • Zoomed out: names give way to family labels. Tap a label to open that family: you'll see their children, and each child's family stays folded until you tap it too. The GEN markers on the left show which generation you're looking at, and the small map in the corner shows where you are.
    • Save as image: downloads a picture of the tree as it's showing now (folded branches stay folded), ready for the family group chat.

    Adding to the tree

    This is the part we need you for. Send whatever you know about someone:

    • their full name, and what people call them
    • how they fit in (whose child, partner or parent they are)
    • birth and death years, if you know them
    • a photo, if you have one
    • a sentence or two of story: where they lived, what they did, something people remember

    Don't know everything? Send it anyway. Blanks are fine, and anything we're unsure about goes in pencil until someone confirms it.

    Updating it yourself

    For whoever ends up keeping the tree. The whole family lives in one list at the top of this file, under the heading EDIT THE FAMILY HERE. The tree draws itself from that list, so you never have to move anything by hand.

    1. Make a copy of the file first, so there's always a backup.
    2. Open the file in a plain text editor (TextEdit set to plain text, Notepad, or VS Code), not a word processor.
    3. Find the list and copy one person, from { to },. Paste it as a new entry and change the details.
    4. Connect them: list their parents' ids in parents, or add their id to a partner's partners.
    5. Save the file and reload the page. The tree grows them into the right place.

    Each person looks like this:

    {
  id: "first-last",          // short, lowercase, no spaces
  name: "First Last",
  born: "", died: "",        // years or dates; "" if unknown
  photo: "photos/first.jpg", // "" if there's no photo yet
  parents: ["parent-id"],    // 0, 1 or 2 ids from the list
  partners: [],              // ids; only one side needs it
  tentative: false,          // true = draw the link in pencil
  placeholder: false,        // true = an empty "to add" bud
  note: "A sentence or two of story."
},
    • Keep the quotes, commas and brackets exactly as they are. A missing comma is the most common reason the tree won't show up. If that happens, undo your last change.
    • Put photos in a photos folder right next to this file, so they work offline and show up in saved images.
    • The title and the date stamp in the corner are in TREE, just above the list.
